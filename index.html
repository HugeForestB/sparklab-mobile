EXTENSIONS & STANDARDS

Teacher tools & advanced physics

Sixth-grade content comes first. The deeper math and field models are optional and visibly separated.

Choose the explanation layer

Student-first: short definitions, observable changes, reasoning prompts, and a speaker connection. Advanced: teacher notes introducing vector calculus, electromagnetic fields, and geometric interpretations without treating them as sixth-grade requirements.

How to use in class

  1. Ask students to predict before manipulating the model.
  2. Have them change just one variable.
  3. Pause the animation so students can describe what did and did not change.
  4. Connect the explanation to the wireless speaker.
  5. Have students explain orally, in writing, or with a labeled drawing.

Scientific guardrails

  • Small beads stand for exaggerated drift, not physical electron speeds.
  • Electrons never leap across the open gap; the capacitor-like extension only shows transient field changes and charge redistribution.
  • Sound needs a medium, while electromagnetic waves can travel in vacuum.
  • Particles vibrate locally; they do not ride with a traveling wave.
  • Water surface motion can have both vertical and horizontal components.

NCDPI boundaries

Thermal energy transfer topics PS.6.2.1–2.2 do not require thermal-energy calculations; PS.6.2.3 does not require specific insulation-value calculations. PS.6.3.1 does not require calculating wave speed, frequency, or wavelength.

Other scope suggestions in this app are instructional choices, not verbatim boundary statements.

Accessibility & privacy

Mobile and keyboard operation, text explanations, high-contrast diagrams, manual pause, and reduced-motion support. Progress is stored only in this browser via localStorage (where permitted). No login, server, analytics, or third-party resources.

All diagrams are idealized educational models, not laboratory measurements.
